## 1. Company Introduction

Trading Labs is a software product being developed by our company. The company is engaged in **Information Technology — software product development**.

In simple terms, we are building a computer-based research tool for people who study and trade shares listed on the Indian stock market. The product helps users look at market information, shortlist stocks for further study, understand charts and news, test ideas against past market behaviour, and practise trading with virtual (not real) money.

The company is a technology and software business. It is **not** a stockbroker, portfolio manager, lender, or payment company. It does **not** hold customer money or shares, and it does **not** buy or sell shares on the stock exchange on behalf of clients.

## 3. What the Company Is Developing

The company is developing **Trading Labs**, a research and practice-trading workstation for Indian shares.

Many people who wish to study the stock market face a common problem: there are hundreds of listed companies, large volumes of price information, and a constant flow of news. It is difficult to review all of this by hand and to practise decision-making without putting real money at risk.

Trading Labs is being built to address that problem. It is a software product that:

1. Collects publicly available market information on Indian listed shares.
2. Helps the user scan a large list of companies and shortlist a smaller set worth studying.
3. Presents charts, research notes, news, and a suggested study plan for each shortlisted company.
4. Allows the user to test a trading idea against past market data, to see how it would have behaved historically.
5. Allows the user to practise buying and selling with **virtual money** inside the software, so that learning can happen without using real funds.

The software is **advisory and educational in nature**. It helps a user do their own research. It does not place real trades, and it is not personalised investment advice from a registered investment adviser.

## 4. Key Functions of the Product

The product has five main functions, described below in everyday language.

### 4.1 Market information

The software displays current and historical price information for Indian listed shares. This information is obtained from a licensed Indian stockbroker’s market-data service and is used only for research and for practice trading inside the software.

Users can view market overviews, price charts, and related company information on a simple dashboard.

### 4.2 Stock scanning and shortlisting

The Indian market has hundreds of listed companies. The software can scan a defined group of shares (for example, the Nifty 500) using clear research rules, such as:

- shares showing a short-term upward trend (swing-style study);
- shares showing longer-term strength (momentum-style study);
- shares making a new 52-week high (breakout-style study).

The result is a ranked shortlist. Each name is labelled in research terms such as **Buy candidate**, **Watch**, or **Reject**. These labels are study aids. They are not instructions to place real orders, and they do not move any real money.

Users can save scan settings, review past scans, and keep a personal watchlist of companies they wish to follow.

### 4.3 Company-level research

When a user selects a company from the shortlist, the software opens a research view. This view brings together:

- a summary of the research view on that share;
- charts and trend information;
- a suggested study plan (possible entry area, safety exit, and target area);
- relevant news, where available;
- a review of how a similar idea would have performed in the past.

The purpose is to give the user one place to study a share before deciding, on their own, whether to take any real-world action outside the software.

### 4.4 Strategy testing

Users can define or import a set of research rules and test those rules on stored price history. This is similar to asking: *“If I had followed this idea in the past, what would have happened?”*

This function is for learning and evaluation only. It does not execute real trades.

### 4.5 Practice trading (virtual money)

Each registered user can have a **practice account** inside the software. This account starts with virtual capital (a simulated balance, not real rupees). The user may:

- place practice buy and sell orders;
- track open practice positions;
- see practice profit and loss;
- review trade history and performance reports.

Practice trades use live market prices for realism. They are **not** sent to a stockbroker as real exchange orders. They do **not** transfer real money. They do **not** result in this company, or the user through this company, buying or selling actual shares.

User access is protected by login (email and password, with optional Google sign-in). An administrator can manage user access. The software stores research history and practice-trading records for the user’s own review.

## 5. Business Purpose

The business purpose of the company is as follows.

**Primary purpose.** To develop, operate, and, in due course, offer a software product that helps individuals research Indian listed shares more efficiently and practise trading decisions without using real money.

**How value is created.** The software reduces the time and effort required to scan the market, organise research, and learn through practice. It is a research and education tool, not a brokerage service.

**Intended commercial model.** When the product is commercially offered, the company expects to earn from:

- software subscriptions;
- software licences;
- related information-technology or support services.

The company does not intend to earn brokerage commission, manage client portfolios, lend money, or hold customer deposits.

**Use of the Current Account.** The Current Account is required for the company’s own legitimate business receipts and operating expenses, including:

- founder capital introduced into the business;
- future software, subscription, licence, or IT-service receipts;
- payments for cloud hosting, market-data services, software tools, professional fees, contractors, and other ordinary operating costs.

Transactions are expected to be mainly digital (NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, and bank transfers). Cash, if any, will be limited to ordinary small business expenses.
